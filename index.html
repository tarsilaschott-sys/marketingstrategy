// 01

What the brand
already has right

The brand strategy built to date is genuinely strong. The SEVERE framework, the Cult Document, the Palantir-aligned positioning — most fintech companies never get this close to a coherent identity. The core line is real:

"We are not building an investing app.
We are building better investors."
intelligentinvesting.ai

The enemy is correctly defined. The identity offer is clear. The visual system is distinctive. The problem is not the brand.

The problem is there is no funnel. We have doctrine without distribution — a philosophy without a mechanism for the right people to find it. That is what this strategy fixes.

The one tension to resolve

Strategic Conflict

The SEVERE Marketing Strategy (February) states 1 post per week maximum, never more than 1 post in 7 days. The KARP Strategy (current) states 1 post per day.


These are not compatible. Daily posting will make this account look like every other finance account within 30 days. The SEVERE document has the better argument. The resolution: batch content production bi-weekly, publish selectively. Content is produced in concentrated sessions every two weeks — not daily. This creates a full queue without daily effort from the marketing function, while the publishing cadence remains controlled and intentional.

The governing principle: "If marketing does not increase desirability, we do not do it. We are not indifferent to uptake. We are indifferent to uptake that does not compound."

Who this is
actually for

The strategy documents describe the target audience correctly in philosophy but not precisely enough for distribution. Precision matters because every channel decision, content decision, and tone decision depends on knowing exactly who you are speaking to.

Primary
The Aspirational Disciplined Investor
28–45 years old. Has investable capital but feels they're making mistakes they cannot name. Distrusts their own impulses. Reads about investing but gets noise, not signal. Self-identifies as serious — not as a trader or retail investor. Responds to being respected, not coached.
Target
Secondary
The Frustrated Professional
Has money. Has financial literacy. Has been burned by complexity. Wants a system they can trust and stop thinking about. Would describe Intelligent Investing to a colleague as: "it's the serious version."
Target

Who this is not for

  • Day traders, crypto enthusiasts, people who want excitement
  • People who need constant reassurance during volatility
  • People who want a community, a coach, or a forum
  • Anyone who would describe good investing as "making moves"

Why this matters for channel selection: The primary audience is on Instagram (consuming, not only posting), on LinkedIn professionally, and reads long-form newsletters. They are not primarily on X/Twitter anymore, and they are definitively not on TikTok.

// 03

Channel architecture

The original strategy documents recommend X/Twitter as the primary signal channel. This recommendation is revised. X's algorithm has become less predictable, organic reach has dropped for non-viral content, and the platform competes with crypto speculation and market noise — precisely what this brand stands against. The serious investor this brand targets has largely migrated.

The channel the strategy documents ignore entirely: LinkedIn. This is a mistake that this strategy corrects. A post stating "Most investors underperform because they cannot sit still" lands with entirely different authority on LinkedIn — surrounded by professional context — than it does on X surrounded by chaos.

// 04

Content strategy

The four-category content architecture from the KARP Strategy is correct and is retained. Every piece of content belongs to one of these categories and follows the three-part post structure: Hard Claim → System Explanation → Correction.

Category 01
System Failure
Expose the industry. The system rewards behaviour that destroys returns. Name it without apology.
Category 02
Behavioural Edge
Rewire thinking. Address the psychological patterns that produce underperformance. Not advice. Diagnosis.
Category 03
Capital Discipline
What to actually do. Concrete. Uncomfortable. Non-consensus. Not a tip. A position.
Category 04
Identity
Who this is for. Filter content. Repel the wrong audience. Attract the rare investor who recognises themselves.

Content formats and their purpose

Format Channel Purpose Frequency Who executes
Reels Instagram Discovery and reach. The algorithm distributes Reels to non-followers. Primary mechanism for audience growth. Black/white, text-only animation — severe aesthetic, not entertainment aesthetic. 3 × / week Agent → Claude Design → Polish
Carousels Instagram + LinkedIn Depth and saves. The highest-saved format. Takes a single idea from the post structure and expands it across 5–7 slides. Becomes a brand artefact people return to. 1–2 × / week Agent → Claude Design or Figma
Static Post Instagram Brand statement. Single truth. Institutional. Used sparingly so it retains weight when it appears. 1 × / week Agent → Claude Design
Text Post LinkedIn Thought leadership and direct distribution to professional audience. Longer first line than Instagram. Same philosophy, calibrated for professional context. 3–4 × / week Agent (adapted from Instagram concepts)
The Edge Email One idea. Timeless. Reads like internal correspondence. No greeting. Ends with a principle, not a goodbye. Converts consideration to conviction. Weekly Agent → Light editorial review

The Reel production workflow

This workflow already exists in practice. The agent reduces effort at the front end so the judgment steps — the ones that must remain human — get more attention, not less.

Step 01
Agent generates
  • Concept + hard claim
  • Full 3-part copy
  • Visual brief for Claude Design
  • Midjourney image brief (if B&W photo reel)
  • Caption copy
Step 02
Human reviews
  • Review concepts (15 min batch)
  • Approve or flag
  • Generate Midjourney image if needed
  • Bring brief to Claude Design
Step 03
Claude Design produces
  • 3 visual / animation options
  • CEO + Marketing select one
  • Light polish applied
Step 04
Compliance + Schedule
  • Submit to compliance
  • Schedule upon approval
  • Publish
Step 05
Agent analyses results
  • Saves, reach, profile visits
  • Which categories performed
  • Which visual mode resonated
  • Feeds insights back to Step 01

On visual tone for Reels: Two modes. The default is black background with white text appearing sequentially — institutional, severe, no music. For a portion of Reels, documentary-style black and white photography is used as background — still images generated in Midjourney using a visual brief produced by the content agent. The agent specifies what image to generate (composition, subject, mood) so the Midjourney prompt is already written. White text overlaid on the photograph. Both modes maintain the same visual severity — the image is never the point, the statement is.

// 05

The compliance pipeline

All content requires compliance review before publication. This is a non-negotiable constraint that defines the entire production rhythm. Content cannot be created on the same week it publishes. The minimum viable buffer is two weeks ahead at all times.

Week A — Production
Generate Week C content
  • Agent generates full week
  • Marketing reviews + approves
  • Visuals executed in Claude Design
  • Submit to compliance by Friday
Week B — Review
Week C in compliance
  • Week C under review
  • Generate Week D content in parallel
  • Execute visuals for Week D
Week C — Publish
Week C goes live
  • Compliance-approved content scheduled
  • Week E now in production
  • Queue never runs dry
Airtable
Pipeline tracker
  • Draft → Review → Approved
  • Scheduled → Published
  • Compliance dates tracked
  • Format + channel logged

Recommended production cadence

Rather than weekly production sessions, produce in bi-weekly batches. One two-hour session every two weeks generates all content for the following two publish weeks. This reduces context-switching, allows better thematic coherence, and ensures the compliance queue is always full with buffer.

Session Output Effort (Marketing) Effort (CEO)
Bi-weekly batch (2 hrs) 14 days of content across all formats and channels 30 min review + visual execution in Claude Design Pick 1 of 3 Reel options per concept
Compliance submission Week 1 batch submitted 15 min admin None
Scheduling Approved content scheduled across platforms 20 min None

The funnel

Currently, there is no clear funnel architecture. There is a brand and a product, but no defined mechanism for moving someone from "never heard of this" to "paying subscriber." The critical missing piece is the bridge between consideration and conviction — that bridge is The Edge newsletter.

01
Awareness
A Reel, a LinkedIn post, or a mirrored X post reaches someone who fits the profile. They stop scrolling.
Instagram Reels · LinkedIn · X
02
Recognition
Something they read stops them. They recognise themselves in it. They check the account. They read more.
Account profile · Carousels · Archive
03
Consideration
They visit the website. They find the philosophy. They find the Constraints page. They read "Proceed Deliberately."
intelligentinvesting.ai · Constraints page
04
Conviction
They subscribe to The Edge — free, frictionless. Over 4 editions they begin thinking of themselves as an II-type investor. Identity is pre-committed before the product.
The Edge newsletter — free entry point
05
Conversion
Signing up for the product formalises what they already believe about themselves. $20/month. No friction required — the identity did the work.
App — Managed or Self-directed
06
Advocacy
No referral incentives. They send it to one person who is exactly like them. That is the only referral that compounds.
Silent referral — identity recognition

AI agents

Each channel has distinct content requirements. A single agent producing copy for all channels will produce generic output. The architecture below assigns one agent per content function, each trained on the brand voice and the specific format conventions of its channel.

Agent 01
Content Concept Engine
Generates weekly batch of concepts across all four categories. The upstream source that feeds all other agents.
Input Category, week number, topic constraints
Output 7–10 concepts with core idea, hard claim, and one-line brief each
Agent 02
Instagram Reel Writer
Takes a concept and produces the full Reel brief — script, visual direction for Claude Design, and caption.
Input Concept from Agent 01
Output Opening claim, 3-part body, visual brief, caption copy
Agent 03
Carousel Writer
Expands a single idea across 5–7 slides. Works for both Instagram and LinkedIn document format. Slide-by-slide copy, ready for Claude Design or Figma.
Input Concept from Agent 01
Output Cover slide + 5–7 body slides + closing slide, with copy for each
Agent 04
LinkedIn Adaptor
Takes the same concept used for Instagram and produces a LinkedIn-native version. Same idea, calibrated for professional context — longer opening, slightly more grounded, same severity.
Input Instagram concept + caption from Agent 02
Output LinkedIn post copy — no hashtags, ends with a principle
Agent 05
The Edge Newsletter Writer
Drafts a weekly newsletter edition. One idea. Timeless. No greeting. No sign-off implying conversation. Ends with a principle. Feels like internal correspondence, not a newsletter.
Input Weekly topic from a rolling 12-week topic map
Output Subject line + full body + closing signature. ~200–300 words.
Agent 06
Braze Automation
Handles The Edge send automatically once the newsletter copy is approved. Connects to the Braze REST API. Zero manual effort after setup. Requires Braze API key with send permissions.
Input Approved newsletter copy + send date
Output Automated send to full subscriber list via Braze
Agent 07
Performance & Insights Analyst
Pulls performance data weekly from Instagram and LinkedIn. Analyses what is working — by content category, by visual mode (black background vs. documentary B&W photo), by format. Produces a concise insight brief and feeds recommendations directly back to the Content Concept Engine (Agent 01). Marketing does not review raw metrics. The agent translates data into production decisions.
Input Instagram Graph API + LinkedIn API — saves, reach, profile visits, follower quality signals
Output Weekly Slack brief every Monday 8am. Top performers by category, visual mode comparison, format breakdown, production recommendation for next batch. Logged to Airtable.
Agent 08
Competitor Intelligence
Scans competitor social accounts, messaging, content formats, and product positioning bi-weekly. Output is not content inspiration — it is positioning awareness. Two sections only: what the field is crowded on, and where the white space is. Informs what to avoid, not what to do.
Input Competitor Instagram, LinkedIn accounts + web — Wealthsimple, Betterment, Robinhood, relevant finance content creators
Output Bi-weekly Slack brief (1st and 3rd Monday of month). What the field is crowded on. Where the white space is. Action required or not.
Agent 09
Market Scanner
Scans market conditions weekly through a structural lens — not news, not events, but persistent patterns that confirm or challenge the brand's thesis. Output always answers one question: does current market behaviour validate our positioning? The answer is almost always yes. That consistency is the point — it trains the team that the philosophy holds across conditions, and pre-empts reactive content decisions.
Input Financial market data, retail trading volume signals, macro indicators — filtered through a structural pattern lens, not a news lens
Output Weekly Slack brief every Friday morning. One structural observation. Noise filter verdict. Whether current conditions feed into next content batch. Never reactive.
Agent 10
Social Format Intelligence
Monitors what formats, lengths, and distribution patterns are performing for non-entertainment accounts in the finance and investing category. Output is structural only — never content inspiration, never trend-chasing. Answers: how should we structure and distribute what we have already decided to say.
Input Instagram Insights API, LinkedIn analytics — Reel length performance, carousel save rates, posting window data, format benchmarks by category
Output Bi-weekly Slack brief (alternating Mondays with Agent 08). Format recommendations only — length, timing, slide count. Explicit list of what to ignore.
// 08

The intelligence channel

All four intelligence agents (07–10) deliver their output to a single Slack channel: #ii-marketing-intel. CEO and marketing are the only members. It is not a conversation channel — it is a briefing channel. Agents post, humans read.

This exists for two reasons. The first is operational: all performance, competitive, market, and format intelligence is consolidated in one place, always current, never requiring a meeting or a status request. The second is strategic: a CEO who receives structured weekly briefs does not need to ask for updates. The briefs answer the question before it is asked. Over time, the consistent message — our thesis holds, no reactive action required — conditions the right response to market pressure.

Setup required: One Slack Incoming Webhook URL (5 minutes to create in Slack workspace settings). No MCP connector needed. Agents POST directly to the webhook. The webhook URL is added to agent configuration once and never touched again.

Delivery schedule

Agent Brief Frequency Delivery Why this cadence
Agent 07 Performance Brief Weekly Monday 8am Feeds directly into the bi-weekly production session. CEO sees results before the week begins.
Agent 08 Competitor Brief Bi-weekly 1st and 3rd Monday Competitors move fast enough that monthly leaves gaps. Bi-weekly keeps the CEO feeling the field is watched.
Agent 09 Market Brief Weekly Friday morning CEO goes into the weekend informed. Weekly frequency trains the expectation that the answer is usually "hold" — over time this reduces reactive pressure, not increases it.
Agent 10 Social Format Brief Bi-weekly 2nd and 4th Monday Alternates with Agent 08 so Mondays always have an intelligence brief. Format data moves slowly enough that bi-weekly is sufficient.

Measurement

Measurement is handled by Agent 07 — not by the marketing function. The agent pulls data, translates it into production decisions, and logs everything to Airtable. Marketing receives a brief, not a dashboard. The metrics that matter are slower, harder to fake, and directly correlated with revenue — they require interpretation, not observation.

What to ignore What to measure instead Why it matters
Follower count Quality of new followers — are they finance professionals, serious investors? 100 right-fit followers outperform 10,000 wrong ones in every conversion metric
Post engagement rate Saves and silent follows. Low comment volume is a signal of correctness. The right audience saves. The wrong audience comments. High comment volume is a red flag.
Website traffic volume Time on site. Pages per session. Edge newsletter signups. A serious investor reads the Constraints page, the philosophy, and then subscribes to The Edge. That journey takes time.
App downloads 90-day retention rate and behaviour during drawdowns Downloads measure marketing. Retention measures whether the brand promise is real.
Email open rate Conversion rate from Edge subscriber to paid user The Edge's purpose is to pre-commit identity, then convert. Open rate is vanity. Conversion is signal.

The three milestones that confirm the strategy is working

30–90 Days
The right people are following
New followers include finance professionals, institutional-adjacent investors, and people who describe themselves as long-term. Edge subscribers are growing without paid promotion.
3–6 Months
Referrals without incentives
Users forward The Edge with the message "made me think of you." Paid subscribers are staying past 90 days. People describe the platform accurately on their own.
6–18 Months
The language is adopted
People quote the brand's language back. Users describe the platform as "calming" or "different." Sophisticated investors stop asking about features.

90-day execution plan

The plan below is sequenced for minimum effort from the marketing function while building a system that runs without constant intervention. The first four weeks require the most concentrated effort. After that, the pipeline runs on batch production cycles.

Weeks Priority Output Who
1–2 Foundation Build 4-week content library across all formats. Add Constraints page to website. Add Edge newsletter signup to homepage. Configure Airtable compliance pipeline tracker. Marketing + Dev
3–4 First compliance cycle Submit first 2 weeks of content to compliance. Execute visuals in Claude Design while under review. Publish Week 1 once approved. Marketing
5–8 Rhythm Bi-weekly batch production. Compliance cycle running. Reels, Carousels, LinkedIn, The Edge all live. Monitor quality of new followers and Edge signup rate. Marketing (agent-assisted)
9–12 Review + refill Audit first 8 weeks. Who is following? Are Reels reaching new people? Are Edge subscribers converting to paid? Refill content queue for next 4 weeks. Marketing

Starting point: Before building any agents, the most useful immediate action is to produce the first four weeks of content in one session — so the compliance queue is full before the system needs to run on its own. All agents can be built in parallel.
